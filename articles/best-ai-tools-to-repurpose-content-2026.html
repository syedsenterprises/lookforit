Best AI Tools to Repurpose Content in 2026: One Video or Post, Everywhere

Turn one video, podcast or blog post into a week of content. The best AI repurposing tools in 2026, with real free-plan limits.

You already made the content. The expensive part was the idea, the recording and the first draft. Repurposing is the cheap part: turning that one video, podcast episode or blog post into short clips, quote graphics, newsletters and threads. AI tools now do the finding, the cutting and the reformatting — you keep the judgement about what is worth sharing.

The trick is matching the tool to what you start with. A video clipper will not turn a blog post into a newsletter, and a writing assistant will not cut a podcast into clips. This guide is organised by source format, and every tool is listed in the LookForIt directory. Free tiers are stated honestly, including watermarks and minute caps. Prices and limits were checked against live sources in November 2026.

Starting from long video: cut it into clips

OpusClip — the automatic clipper

OpusClip takes a long video and finds the moments worth clipping, then reframes them vertical, adds captions and scores them for viral potential. It is the leader if your goal is Shorts, Reels and TikToks from a YouTube video or a webinar recording. The free plan gives 60 credits a month, which covers a few clips — enough to test whether your audience watches them. Starter is $15 a month.

OpusClip on LookForIt

Descript — edit by editing the text

Descript's model is different: you edit video and audio by editing the transcript. Delete a sentence from the text and it disappears from the recording; cut the filler words in one pass. That makes it the best tool when you also need to fix the edit before you repurpose it — a rambling webinar becomes a tight ten minutes, and then the clips come out of the cleaned version. The free plan gives 60 media minutes a month with 720p exports and a watermark. Hobbyist is $16 a month on annual billing.

Descript on LookForIt

Kapwing — the browser workhorse

Kapwing runs entirely in the browser and covers the whole middle of the job: subtitles, resizing, templates, trimming and repurposing in one place. Its free plan gives watermarked exports up to four minutes long at 720p, with about 30 minutes of use a month. It does not pick the clip-worthy moments for you the way OpusClip does — you choose the moments and Kapwing makes the production fast. Pro is $16 a month on annual billing.

Kapwing on LookForIt

VEED — subtitles and talking-head clips

VEED is the fastest route to captioned talking-head clips: upload, auto-subtitle, trim, export. Its free plan is watermarked at 720p with about 10 minutes a month. If your content is mostly you talking to camera, VEED's subtitle accuracy and speed matter more than fancy clip-finding. Creator plans start at $9 a month on annual billing.

VEED on LookForIt

CapCut — the strongest free editor

CapCut's free plan is the most generous here: the full editor, plain edits without a watermark, and 1080p exports, with no monthly minute meter on basic editing. It does not find viral moments for you, but for cutting, captioning and reformatting clips by hand it costs nothing. Pro is $15 a month on annual billing. If your budget is zero, start here and add OpusClip when you can pay for the finding step.

CapCut on LookForIt

Starting from a podcast or audio: turn talk into text

Descript, again — the audio-first workflow

The same transcript-editing model works for podcasts: record or upload the episode, edit out the mistakes in the text, then export clips, show notes and social copy from the same transcript. One tool covers the audio edit and the text repurposing, which is why podcasters keep coming back to it.

Riverside — record and clip in one place

Riverside records remote interviews in high quality and now includes AI clipping, so the episode and its short clips come out of the same session. It suits podcasters who record with guests and want clips the same day, without moving files between tools.

Riverside on LookForIt

Starting from a blog post or article: turn text into video and social

Lumen5 — blog post to video

Lumen5 turns a blog post, script or document into a social video by pulling out key sentences and matching them with stock footage. Its free plan gives five videos a month, each up to two minutes, at 720p, with a Lumen5 outro at the end. That is enough for a weekly blog-to-video habit. Basic is $19 a month on annual billing and removes the branding.

Lumen5 on LookForIt

Fliki — scripts into narrated video

Fliki turns scripts and blog posts into narrated videos with AI voices in more than 80 languages, plus stock visuals. Its free plan gives three credits a month — about five minutes of audio and video — with a watermark and no commercial use, so it is an audition, not a publishing plan. Paid plans start at $21 a month on annual billing. The voice quality is the reason to try it: for explainer content and multilingual versions of the same video, it is a clear step above robotic narration.

Fliki on LookForIt

Pictory — trial only, and worth knowing that

Pictory has no free plan — only a 14-day free trial with 300 credits. It turns articles, scripts and long recordings into captioned social videos, and it is good at it, but budget for Starter at $25 a month on annual billing if you want to keep using it. Do not build a workflow around a trial.

Pictory on LookForIt

ChatGPT and Claude — text into every other text format

For the text side of repurposing, the general assistants do the most work. Give ChatGPT or Claude a blog post and ask for a LinkedIn post, a thread, a newsletter section, a video script and five quote graphics' worth of pull-quotes — in one pass. ChatGPT's free tier covers everyday text chats, and Claude's free tier gives Sonnet access with limits that reset every five hours. The quality depends entirely on the brief: paste the source, name the audience, name the format, and ask for one format at a time.

ChatGPT on LookForIt · Claude on LookForIt

Jasper — marketing copy at scale, trial only

Jasper has no free tier, only a free trial, so it sits in the paid column. Its value for repurposing is brand voice: feed it your style once, and every repurposed post sounds like your organisation rather than like a template. If you only repurpose occasionally, ChatGPT or Claude free will do the same job.

Jasper on LookForIt

Starting from visuals: resize and reformat

Canva — one design, every size

Canva's free tier includes over 1.6 million templates and basic AI tools, and its resize function turns one graphic into every platform's size in a click. A quote graphic from your blog post becomes a story, a feed post, a Pinterest pin and a thumbnail without rebuilding it four times. Canva Pro is free for verified nonprofits.

Canva on LookForIt

Gamma — long text into a deck

Gamma turns a blog post or an outline into a slide deck, document or simple web page. Its free plan gives 400 AI credits once at sign-up — they never refill — enough for a few short decks. Useful when a blog post deserves a second life as a presentation or a lead magnet. Plus starts at $9 a month on annual billing.

Gamma on LookForIt

Distribution: stop posting the same thing by hand

Zapier and Make — automate the shipping

Once the repurposed pieces exist, automation ships them. Zapier's free plan gives 100 tasks a month on two-step Zaps — a new YouTube video triggers a post, a new blog post adds a row to a sheet. Make's free plan gives 1,000 credits a month with multi-step scenarios, which is far more generous if you can handle the learning curve. Both are the difference between repurposing as a habit and repurposing as a one-off project.

Zapier on LookForIt · Make on LookForIt · n8n on LookForIt

Three workflows that actually work

YouTube video to a week of Shorts. Upload the long video to OpusClip and take the three highest-scored clips. Clean the captions in CapCut free. Post one a day, and use Claude to turn the video's key points into a thread on the same day the first clip goes out.

Podcast episode to blog and social. Edit the episode in Descript by cutting the transcript. Export the cleaned transcript, give it to ChatGPT with the instruction "turn this into a blog post, keep the quotes verbatim", then ask for five social posts from the same transcript. One recording, one blog post, five posts.

Blog post to video and newsletter. Paste the post into Lumen5 for a two-minute social video. Give the same post to Claude and ask for a newsletter version with a different opening. Resize the post's header image in Canva for every platform. The post now exists in four formats from one afternoon's work.

The free-tier reality check

Free plans in this category are honest but narrow: minute caps, watermarks, 720p exports and no commercial use on the cheapest tiers. The pattern to remember is that finding the moment (OpusClip), editing (Descript, CapCut) and distributing (Zapier, Make) are three different jobs, and most tools are strong at one and adequate at the others. A $0 stack that covers all three is CapCut for editing, Claude free for the text, and Make free for distribution — then add OpusClip when you can pay for the finding step.

One more caution: platforms deprioritise lazy reposts. Since March 2026, Facebook has downranked reaction-only reels and clip stitches that add nothing but borders or captions. A repurposed clip needs a new caption, a new hook or a new context — the AI can cut it, but the reason to watch still has to come from you.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free AI tool to repurpose content?

There is no single best tool — it depends on your source. For long video, OpusClip's free tier finds the clips. For editing, CapCut free is the most generous. For text, ChatGPT and Claude free do the most work. For distribution, Make free covers the most volume.

Can I repurpose content for free?

Yes, with limits: watermarks, 720p exports, minute caps and no commercial use on some free tiers. Fliki's free exports, for example, cannot legally be used on a monetised channel. Check the licence on any free tier before you publish.

Is Pictory free?

No. Pictory has a 14-day free trial only — there is no permanent free plan. Lumen5 and Fliki are the free alternatives for blog-to-video.

How much content should one video become?

A good rule is one long video to three short clips, one thread, one newsletter section and one quote graphic. More than that and you are repeating yourself; less and you are leaving reach on the table.

Prices and free-tier limits change often. Check each vendor's pricing page before you rely on a number. Figures in this article were checked in November 2026.
