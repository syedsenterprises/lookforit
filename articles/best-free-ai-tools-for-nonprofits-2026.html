Best Free AI Tools for Nonprofits in 2026 (Tested Free Tiers, Real Discounts)

Free AI tools for nonprofits in 2026: writing, design, meetings and automation that cost $0, plus the nonprofit discounts worth claiming.

Most nonprofit teams run on budgets close to zero, so the only AI tools that matter are the ones that do real work for free — or come with a verified nonprofit discount. This guide covers exactly that. Every tool below is listed in the LookForIt directory, and every free tier and discount mentioned was checked against live sources in November 2026. Where a tool is trial-only rather than free, we say so plainly.

One warning before the list: free consumer AI tools may use what you type to train their models. Never paste donor names, addresses, gift amounts or other personal data into a free chatbot. Use these tools for drafts and public-facing copy, and keep donor records in your own systems.

The $0 starter stack

If you only set up five things this week, make it these: ChatGPT or Claude for writing, Canva for design, Otter.ai for meeting notes, Zapier or Make for automation, and Google for Nonprofits for the whole Workspace setup. That combination covers newsletters, appeals, social posts, grant drafts, board minutes and donation follow-ups without spending anything.

Writing and drafting

ChatGPT — the everyday writing assistant

ChatGPT's free tier covers everyday text chats with no fixed message count published, which makes it the easiest place to start. Use it for first drafts of newsletters, appeal letters, volunteer thank-yous, social posts and event descriptions — then edit the output in your own voice before anything goes out. Paid plans start with Go at $8 a month and Plus at $20 a month, but a small team can run a long way on the free tier. OpenAI also offers discounted Team plans for nonprofits.

ChatGPT on LookForIt

Claude — the careful editor

Claude's free tier gives access to its Sonnet and Haiku models, with usage limits that reset every five hours and weekly. Its strength for nonprofits is long documents: paste in a full grant draft or an annual report and ask for structure, repetition and clarity problems — it holds long context well and follows instructions closely. Pro is $20 a month, or $17 a month on the annual plan. Anthropic offers large discounts on Team and Enterprise plans for nonprofits.

Claude on LookForIt

Google Gemini — best if you already live in Google Workspace

Gemini's free tier works inside Gmail, Docs and Sheets, so drafting and research happen where your files already are. The bigger win is the Google for Nonprofits programme: eligible nonprofits get Google Workspace free, Gemini for Workspace at no cost, and up to $10,000 a month in Google Ad Grants. If your organisation has not applied for Google for Nonprofits yet, that single application is worth more than any individual tool on this list.

Google Gemini on LookForIt

Grammarly — polish for everything you publish

Grammarly's free plan covers core grammar and spelling checks plus 100 AI prompts a month, which is enough to clean up donor emails, press releases and website copy. It runs in the browser and in Word and Google Docs, so volunteers can use it without training. Pro starts at $12 a month per member.

Grammarly on LookForIt

Design and visual content

Canva — and the Canva for Nonprofits programme

Canva's free tier is genuinely useful, with over 1.6 million templates, basic AI tools and real-time collaboration. But the real prize is Canva for Nonprofits: verified nonprofits get Canva Pro completely free for up to 50 users. That unlocks the full Magic Studio AI suite — background removal, Magic Write, brand kits — for flyers, annual reports, social graphics and fundraising campaign pages. Apply with your charity registration documents; approval is the only step that costs time.

Canva on LookForIt

Meetings, notes and follow-ups

Otter.ai — meeting transcription on a free plan

Otter's free Basic plan includes 300 transcription minutes a month, with a 30-minute limit per conversation. That covers a weekly team meeting or a monthly board call, with a live transcript, speaker identification and a searchable record afterwards. The catch is the 30-minute cap — an hour-long board meeting will be cut off — and only your 25 most recent conversations are kept. Pro is $8.33 a user a month on annual billing and raises the limits to 1,200 minutes a month.

Otter.ai on LookForIt

Fireflies.ai — the alternative when meetings run long

Fireflies' free plan lists unlimited transcription, but storage is capped at 400 minutes per team and AI summaries are limited — and for accounts created on or after 1 September 2026, free transcription is limited to three meetings unless Auto-join is switched on. Where it beats Otter is the per-meeting length: recordings run up to two hours, so a full board meeting fits. It also pushes notes into Slack, Notion, HubSpot and other tools. Pro is $10 a seat a month on annual billing.

Fireflies.ai on LookForIt

Organising the work

Notion — honest note on the AI

Notion itself is free for individuals and works well as a shared knowledge base: grant deadlines, programme notes, volunteer handbooks and meeting archives in one searchable place. But Notion AI is not free — the Free and Plus plans only get a limited trial of AI features, and full Notion AI needs the Business plan at $20 a seat a month on annual billing. Use Notion free for organisation, and do the AI writing in ChatGPT or Claude instead.

Notion AI on LookForIt

Microsoft Copilot — best for Microsoft 365 shops

If your organisation already runs on Microsoft 365, Copilot Chat is included at no extra cost for users with an eligible Microsoft 365 subscription. Microsoft also runs a nonprofit programme with heavily discounted licences. It is the same job as Gemini for Google shops: draft inside the tools you already pay for, rather than adding a new subscription.

Microsoft Copilot on LookForIt

Automation: stop copying data by hand

Zapier — the simple connector

Zapier's free plan gives 100 tasks a month on two-step Zaps — one trigger, one action. That is enough for the highest-value nonprofit automation: a new donation triggers a thank-you email, a new form response adds a row to a sheet, a new volunteer signs up and gets a welcome message. The limit to know is that webhooks and multi-step workflows need a paid plan, which starts at $19.99 a month on annual billing.

Zapier on LookForIt

Make — the generous free tier

Make's free plan gives 1,000 credits a month with multi-step scenarios allowed, which is roughly ten times the volume of Zapier's free tier for the same $0. The trade-off is a steeper learning curve — you build visual scenarios with routers and filters rather than simple two-step Zaps. For a nonprofit with one tech-confident volunteer, Make covers donor follow-ups, event reminders and report generation free. Core is $9 a month on annual billing for 10,000 credits.

Make on LookForIt

n8n — free if you self-host

n8n's Community Edition is free and open source if you host it yourself, with no usage limits. That suits nonprofits with a volunteer who can run a small server, and it keeps donor data on your own infrastructure — a real advantage when you cannot paste personal data into cloud AI tools. The hosted cloud version starts at €20 a month.

n8n on LookForIt

Presentations and pitches

Gamma — funder decks in minutes

Gamma turns a prompt or an outline into a slide deck, document or simple web page. The free plan gives 400 AI credits once at sign-up — they never refill — which is enough for roughly four to six short decks. That covers a funder pitch, a board update and a volunteer onboarding deck. Paid plans start at $9 a month on annual billing.

Gamma on LookForIt

Marketing copy: one honest exception

Jasper — trial only, but with a nonprofit discount

Jasper has no free tier — only a free trial — so it does not belong in the free list above. It earns a mention because Jasper offers nonprofits a 20% discount after the trial, and its brand-voice features help a small team keep every appeal and campaign page sounding like the same organisation. Try it only if the free tools above are not covering your marketing copy.

A week-one plan

Day one, apply for Google for Nonprofits and Canva for Nonprofits — those two applications unlock the most value. Day two, set up ChatGPT or Claude for drafting and Grammarly for proofing. Day three, connect Otter.ai or Fireflies.ai to your regular meetings. Day four, build one Zapier or Make automation — donation to thank-you email is the best first one. Day five, make your next funder deck in Gamma. Nothing on this list needs a credit card to start, and the only thing that costs money is the moment your volume outgrows a free tier — which is a good problem to have.

Frequently asked questions

Is ChatGPT really free for nonprofits?

The free tier is free for everyone, including nonprofits. OpenAI also offers discounted Team plans for registered nonprofits — apply through OpenAI rather than assuming the discount applies automatically.

Can we use free AI tools with donor data?

No — not for personal data. Free consumer tools may use your inputs to train their models. Use them for public copy and drafts, and keep donor names, addresses and gift amounts in your own CRM.

What is the single best free AI tool for a small nonprofit?

Canva for Nonprofits, once verified — free Pro for up to 50 users covers design, presentations and social content. For writing, start with ChatGPT or Claude free.

Do we need to pay for grant writing AI?

No. ChatGPT and Claude on their free tiers draft strong first passes from your programme details. A human must still own the final text — funders can tell when an application was written entirely by a chatbot, and accuracy matters more than speed.

Prices and free-tier limits change often. Check each vendor's pricing page before you rely on a number. Figures in this article were checked in November 2026.
