How to Choose an AI Tool in October 2026 Without Falling for Fake Ratings

New AI tools appear every week, and many directories pad their lists with scraped entries and invented ratings. Use this 5-step check before you pay.

The problem with most AI tool lists

Every week brings a new wave of AI tools, and most directories grow by scraping. A scraped list can show a tool that no longer exists, a price that was never charged, or a rating that no user ever gave. If a directory will not say how a listing got there, treat its counts with suspicion.

LookForIt takes the opposite route, and it is worth stating plainly because it is the site's whole point: every listing is added only after a human opens the tool, checks what it actually does, and confirms its official website works. The directory lists 113 verified AI tools at the time of writing, plus 20 free tools that run entirely in your browser — no sign-up, no downloads, nothing sent anywhere. If a price could not be verified, the listing says so rather than inventing one.

The 5-step check before you pay for any AI tool

1. Open the official site yourself. A listing is a lead, not a verdict. Click through to the maker's own site and confirm the tool still exists, still does what the listing says, and still offers the plan you want. Dead links and renamed products are the first sign of a scraped list.

2. Ask how the price was verified. If a directory shows an exact price, it should be able to point to the maker's pricing page. If it cannot, assume the price is stale. Trial first: never commit to an annual plan on a listing alone.

3. Ignore star ratings and review counts you cannot trace. A rating without named reviewers, a date, and a source is decoration. Compare tools on what they do — input, output, limits, and export — not on a 4.9 that nobody can audit.

4. Test the free version on your real task. Do not test with a toy prompt. Run the exact job you bought it for — your document, your image style, your code, your voice sample — and check the output, the limits, and whether you can export your work.

5. Check the exit before the entrance. Can you cancel without emailing support? Can you export or delete your data? A tool that is easy to join and hard to leave is expensive even when it is cheap.

Where LookForIt's free tools fit

If a free browser tool already does the job, you have saved a subscription.

A practical shortlist method

  1. Write down the one job, in one sentence.
  2. Browse one category only — productivity, coding, writing, image and video, voice, or business — and pick at most three verified listings.
  3. Run the 5-step check above on those three.
  4. Pay for one, for one month, and set a calendar reminder to cancel or renew on purpose.

That discipline matters more than any single recommendation, because the best tool in October 2026 will not be the best tool in six months. Directories should help you re-check, not lock you into last month's winner.
