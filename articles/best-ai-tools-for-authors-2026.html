Best AI Tools for Authors in 2026: Draft, Edit and Publish a Book

The best AI tools for authors in 2026, by stage: drafting, editing, research, audiobooks and publishing — with honest free-tier limits.

Writing a book is at least five different jobs — outlining, drafting, editing, researching, and then publishing and promoting — and no single AI tool does all of them well. The tools below are organised by the stage they actually solve, and every one is listed in the LookForIt directory. Free tiers are stated honestly, including where a tool is trial-only. Prices and limits were checked against live sources in November 2026.

One rule first: AI can draft, organise and polish, but it cannot be the author. Your voice, your judgement and your facts are the book. Amazon's Kindle Direct Publishing also requires you to disclose AI-generated text, and enforcement tightened in 2026 — undisclosed AI-generated titles are being pulled from sale more aggressively. AI used only to brainstorm or edit your own writing does not need the same disclosure, but check KDP's current guidelines before you publish.

Outlining and drafting

ChatGPT — the brainstorming partner

ChatGPT's free tier covers everyday text chats with no fixed message count published, which makes it the cheapest way to test whether AI helps your process at all. It is strongest at the jobs around the manuscript rather than the prose itself: chapter outlines, character sketches, plot alternatives, query letters and back-cover copy. Plus is $20 a month and Go is $8 a month; the free tier is enough for outlining and occasional drafting help.

ChatGPT on LookForIt

Claude — the long-manuscript thinker

Claude's free tier gives access to its Sonnet and Haiku models, with usage limits that reset every five hours and weekly. Its advantage for authors is long context: a whole manuscript fits in one conversation, so you can ask about pacing, continuity, a sagging middle act, or whether a character's voice drifts between chapters. That makes it the best of the general assistants for full-book feedback. Pro is $20 a month, or $17 a month on annual billing.

Claude on LookForIt

Editing a finished draft

Grammarly — the everyday proofreader

Grammarly's free plan covers core grammar and spelling checks plus 100 AI prompts a month, running in the browser and inside Word and Google Docs. It catches the errors that survive your own read-throughs and keeps punctuation consistent across a long manuscript. It is a proofreader, not a developmental editor — it will not tell you a chapter is boring. Pro starts at $12 a month per member.

Grammarly on LookForIt

QuillBot — rewriting sentences that do not work

QuillBot's free plan caps paraphrasing at about 100 to 125 words per run in two modes, Standard and Fluency, while grammar checks are unlimited. That is exactly the right shape for an author: paste in a paragraph that reads badly, try three rewrites, keep the best line. Premium is $9.95 a month for unlimited words. Use it for sentences, not chapters — rewriting a whole book through a paraphraser would flatten your voice.

QuillBot on LookForIt

Claude, again — the self-edit pass

Claude earns a second mention because its long context makes it the best self-editing tool on this list. Give it a chapter and ask for specific things: where the pacing drags, where a scene repeats information, where dialogue sounds unlike the character. Ask for one problem at a time — a vague "make this better" gets vague results, while "find every place where the timeline contradicts chapter three" gets useful ones.

Research and fact-checking

Perplexity — research with sources attached

Perplexity answers with citations, which is what non-fiction authors need. Its free tier gives unlimited basic search plus a small daily allowance of Pro searches. Use it to verify dates, names, statistics and quotations before they go into your manuscript — and then still check the primary source yourself, because an AI summary is not a citation.

Perplexity on LookForIt

Google NotebookLM (now Gemini Notebook) — your research, answered

Google renamed NotebookLM to Gemini Notebook in July 2026. The product is unchanged: a research tool that answers only from the documents you upload, not from the open web. Upload your interview transcripts, PDFs, articles and notes, then ask questions and get answers grounded in your own sources — with citations back to the exact passage. The free tier allows 100 notebooks and 50 sources per notebook, each source up to 500,000 words or 200MB. For a non-fiction book built on research, this is the most useful free tool on the list, and it also generates audio overviews of your material.

Google NotebookLM on LookForIt

Consensus and Elicit — for research-heavy non-fiction

Consensus and Elicit search academic papers rather than the web, and both have free tiers. If your book leans on studies — health, psychology, economics, education — these tools find the paper, summarise its findings and show you the evidence, instead of returning a confident-sounding paragraph with no source. They are niche, but for the right book they save days.

Consensus on LookForIt · Elicit on LookForIt

DeepL — translation for multilingual research

DeepL's free tier covers 50,000 characters of text a month, one document a month and image translation up to 1,500 characters a day. If your research includes sources in another language, DeepL's translations are consistently more accurate than general chatbots for published prose.

DeepL on LookForIt

Turning the book into an audiobook

ElevenLabs — narration without a studio

ElevenLabs is the best-known AI voice generator, and its free plan gives 10,000 credits a month — about ten minutes of audio — for personal use, with attribution required and no commercial licence. That is enough to test voices and narrate a sample chapter. The Starter plan is $6 a month. Note the licence: free-tier audio cannot be sold, so a commercial audiobook needs a paid plan. ElevenLabs also runs a publishing programme that pays authors per engaged listener, worth checking once your audiobook exists.

ElevenLabs on LookForIt

Descript — record, edit and publish spoken audio

Descript lets you edit audio and video by editing the transcript — delete a sentence from the text and it disappears from the recording. Its free plan gives 60 media minutes a month with 720p exports and a watermark. For authors, the workflow is: record the audiobook chapter by chapter, fix mistakes by editing words, and export. Hobbyist is $16 a month on annual billing.

Descript on LookForIt

Publishing and promotion

Jasper — marketing copy in your voice

Jasper has no free tier — only a free trial — so budget for it or skip it. What it offers authors is brand-voice marketing copy at scale: book descriptions, launch emails, social posts and ad variations that sound like you rather than like a template. If you are self-publishing and doing your own marketing, that consistency matters. If you only need a few pieces of copy, ChatGPT or Claude free will do the same job.

Jasper on LookForIt

Canva — covers, social graphics and launch assets

Canva's free tier includes over 1.6 million templates and basic AI tools, which covers a book cover mock-up, quote graphics, launch announcements and author branding. A professional cover still deserves a professional designer, but Canva handles everything around the cover for free.

Canva on LookForIt

Gamma — pitch decks for agents and publishers

Gamma turns a prompt into a slide deck, which is what you need when pitching an agent or a publisher. The free plan gives 400 AI credits once at sign-up — they never refill — enough for a few short decks. Plus starts at $9 a month on annual billing.

Gamma on LookForIt

A workflow that actually works

Outlining: ChatGPT or Claude free, one conversation per book, with the outline as a living document you revise.

Drafting: Write the prose yourself. Use AI to get unstuck — "give me three ways this scene could end" — not to generate chapters.

Research: NotebookLM for your own sources, Perplexity for facts, Consensus or Elicit for academic claims.

Self-edit: Grammarly for the surface, QuillBot for stubborn sentences, Claude for structure and continuity across the whole manuscript.

Audiobook: ElevenLabs to test voices on a sample chapter, Descript to record and edit, paid plan before you sell anything.

Launch: Canva for graphics, Jasper or ChatGPT for copy, Gamma for the pitch deck.

What AI cannot do for your book

It cannot decide what the book is about, and it cannot tell you whether an idea is true. It will invent quotations, dates and statistics with complete confidence if you let it, so every factual claim needs a human check. It cannot keep a consistent voice across 80,000 words unless you do. And it cannot replace a human editor or beta readers — the people who tell you the middle is slow are worth more than any tool here.

Frequently asked questions

Can I publish a book written by AI?

Amazon KDP requires you to disclose AI-generated text, and enforcement tightened in 2026. AI-assisted editing of your own writing is treated differently from AI-generated content. Check KDP's current content guidelines before publishing.

Which AI tool is best for writing a novel?

For drafting help and full-manuscript feedback, Claude's long context is the strongest free option. For fiction-specific drafting, dedicated novel-writing apps exist, but the general-purpose tools in this guide cover every stage of a book without a subscription.

Is there a completely free way to write a book with AI?

Yes, for the writing itself: ChatGPT free, Claude free, Grammarly free, QuillBot free and NotebookLM free cover outlining, drafting help, editing and research. You only pay when you want a commercial audiobook, a professional cover, or higher usage limits.

Will AI ruin my writing voice?

Only if you let it write for you. Used as a brainstorming partner and an editor, it leaves your voice alone. Used as a ghostwriter, it replaces it — and readers can tell.
