Best AI Tools for Teachers in 2026: Lesson Plans, Feedback, and Differentiation

The best AI tools for teachers in 2026 cut the hours spent on planning, differentiation, and feedback — and most of the useful ones are free for individual teachers. This guide covers the tools that solve a real classroom job, what each free plan actually includes, and the privacy rule to follow before pasting anything student-related.

Start with the privacy rule, not the tool list

Every tool below is easier to adopt once you have one rule in place: never paste student names, grades, photos, or identifiable work into a general chatbot. Education-specific tools are a better default because several of them publish FERPA and COPPA compliance and state that they do not train models on student or teacher data. Even then, your school or district policy wins over any tool's marketing page — if your district has an approved list, start there and use this guide to pick within it.

A practical habit that keeps you safe: draft with placeholder names, check the output yourself, and only then put real class details into the system your school already uses. AI output also needs a human check for facts, reading level, and tone before it reaches students, because a confident-sounding error in a worksheet is worse than a blank one.

Lesson planning: MagicSchool and Khanmigo

MagicSchool is the broadest teacher toolkit on this list. Its Forever Free plan for individual teachers includes more than 80 teacher tools covering lesson planning, worksheet and quiz generation, rubric creation, IEP drafting, and presentation creation, plus Raina, its education chatbot, and class writing feedback. The paid Plus plan costs $12.99 per month, or $8.33 per month billed annually, and adds unlimited generations, unlimited output history, and unlimited editing of AI output. Start on the free plan and upgrade only when a generation or history limit actually interrupts your week. MagicSchool also publishes FERPA, COPPA, GDPR, and SOC 2 compliance, which is the right baseline to ask any classroom tool about.

Khanmigo, from the nonprofit Khan Academy, is the best free option if your class already uses Khan Academy. Its teacher tools are free for eligible educators and cover standards-aligned, differentiated lesson plans, learning objectives, rubrics, exit tickets, problem sets with answer keys, student-group suggestions, progress summaries, and multilingual family communication. Note the boundary: individual teacher access does not itself grant students access to Khanmigo tutoring — classroom student access comes through a school or district arrangement. Learner and parent access is a separate paid product at $4 per month or $44 per year, which teachers do not need for the planning tools.

If you only pick one planning tool, pick MagicSchool for breadth or Khanmigo for depth with Khan Academy content. Teachers who run both usually use MagicSchool for day-to-day drafting and Khanmigo where a lesson ties directly to Khan Academy practice.

Differentiation: Diffit for mixed-ability classes

Diffit solves one classroom problem very well: a single class rarely reads at a single level. Give it a topic, an article URL, a YouTube video, or your own text, and it returns a leveled reading passage with a summary, vocabulary, and ready-made questions, regenerating the same content at different reading levels or in another language. It supports more than 60 languages, which makes it genuinely useful for multilingual learners and intervention groups, not just for one struggling reader.

The free Basic plan lets teachers create, edit, and download or print materials, but it keeps only what you made in the last 90 days and exports to PDF only. The individual plan costs $14.99 per month or $149.99 per year and adds exports to Google Docs, Slides, Forms, and Microsoft formats, standards alignment, and a permanent library. Two honest caveats: independent checks have found leveled passages reading above their labeled grade, so compare versions side by side before handing them out, and Diffit works best as a complement to a core planning tool rather than a replacement for a full unit plan.

Feedback where you already work: Brisk Teaching

Brisk Teaching is a Chrome and Edge extension that runs inside the tools teachers already use — Google Docs, Slides, PDFs, YouTube videos, web articles, and LMS platforms including Google Classroom, Canvas, and Schoology — so there is no tab-switching, copying, or pasting to get help. Its free Forever plan for individual teachers includes more than 20 AI tools: lesson plan generation, reading-level adjustment, quiz generation from a video or article, and feedback on student writing in modes including Glows and Grows, Targeted, Rubric Criteria, and Next Steps. Targeted feedback embeds rubric-aligned comments directly into student work in Google Docs.

Brisk states that it does not use student or teacher data to train or fine-tune AI models, and its premium plans are priced for schools and districts rather than individual teachers, so the free plan is the realistic long-term tier for one teacher. If your day happens in Google Docs and Slides, Brisk is the lowest-friction pick on this list: install the extension, open the document you are already working on, and generate from there.

Interactive lessons: Curipod for live participation

Curipod takes a different angle from printable materials: it generates interactive lessons that students respond to live on their own devices, with slides, polls, word clouds, open-ended questions, and drawing activities, plus AI feedback on writing that students can revise against in real time. It is the right pick when the goal is participation during the lesson rather than a handout to take home.

The free plan covers standard classroom use with weekly writing sessions that renew automatically and unlimited student writers, but it caps student responses at 1,000 characters and limits session frequency and test-prep access. The premium individual plan costs $7.50 per month billed annually or $9 per month billed monthly, and school and district plans are custom-quoted with unlimited sessions, single sign-on, and data privacy agreements. Every student needs a device for the live format, so check your classroom's device reality before building a unit around it.

Slides and classroom visuals: Canva for Education

Canva for Education is 100 percent free for verified K-12 teachers and their students, and Canva commits to keeping it free for them. Teachers sign up with a verified education email domain for instant access, or upload proof of employment such as a teaching licence, with review within a few days; credentials are re-verified every three years. It is not available for higher-education teachers, who are pointed to a separate product, and students gain access through their teacher's invitation rather than signing up alone.

As a design tool first, Canva is strongest for slides, posters, worksheets, and classroom visuals that need to look good, with templates, AI-powered activities, class management, and connections to Google Classroom, Canvas, Schoology, and Microsoft Teams. For AI slide generation specifically, also see Gamma and SlidesAI in the directory. Pair Canva with a planning tool above: plan in MagicSchool or Khanmigo, then design the student-facing version in Canva.

General assistants: ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini

General assistants still earn a place in a teacher's week for flexible drafting: unit outlines, parent emails, sub plans, discussion prompts, and rewording a dense explanation three ways. ChatGPT, Claude, and Google Gemini all work for this, and all have free tiers worth trying before paying. Use them for teacher-side drafting and brainstorming, not as a place to paste identifiable student data, and verify every fact before it reaches a lesson.

Two supporting tools round out the week. Grammarly polishes parent communication, newsletters, and recommendation letters for grammar, clarity, and tone. Otter.ai turns meetings and lectures into searchable notes, and Perplexity answers research questions with links to real pages you can open and verify before citing them in class materials.

Quick comparison: which tool for which job

MagicSchool — best all-round teacher toolkit; free plan with 80+ tools, Plus at $12.99 per month or $8.33 per month billed annually. Khanmigo — best free planning tied to Khan Academy; teacher tools free, learner access $4 per month. Diffit — best differentiation; free Basic plan with 90-day history, individual plan $14.99 per month or $149.99 per year. Brisk — best feedback inside Google Docs and Slides; free plan with 20+ tools, paid plans for schools and districts. Curipod — best live interactive lessons; free plan with weekly sessions, premium $7.50 per month billed annually or $9 per month. Canva for Education — best classroom visuals; 100 percent free for verified K-12 teachers and their students. For tools where a price or limit could not be verified on the vendor's own page at writing time, this guide writes "Pricing not verified here" rather than guessing — re-check the vendor pricing page before budgeting a purchase.

The sensible starting stack costs nothing: MagicSchool for planning, Diffit for leveled readings, Brisk for feedback, and Canva for Education for visuals. Add Khanmigo if you teach with Khan Academy, and Curipod when you want a lesson students respond to live. Pay only when one named limit — generations, exports, history, or sessions — actually slows you down.

Frequently asked questions

Which AI tool is best for teachers overall?

MagicSchool is the best starting point for most teachers because its free plan covers lesson planning, quizzes, rubrics, and feedback in one place. Add Diffit if differentiation is your main problem, and Brisk if your work lives in Google Docs.

Are AI tools really free for teachers?

Several are: MagicSchool, Brisk, and Khanmigo teacher tools all have genuinely free plans, and Canva for Education is 100 percent free for verified K-12 teachers. Free plans usually carry generation, history, or export limits, so check the limit that affects your workflow before relying on one.

Is it safe to put student work into an AI tool?

Do not paste student names, grades, or identifiable work into a general chatbot. Prefer education tools that publish FERPA and COPPA compliance, strip identifiers before pasting anything, and follow your school or district policy, which always wins over a tool's marketing.

Can AI write a whole lesson plan I can trust?

It can draft one fast, but you must check it: objectives against your standard, timing against your period length, and every fact, example, and question before class. Treat the draft as a first version, not a finished plan.

What is the best AI tool for mixed-ability classes?

Diffit is built for exactly that job: it turns one topic, article, or video into reading passages at different levels, with questions and vocabulary, so one class gets tiered materials instead of one text that fits nobody.

Should a teacher pay for an AI tool?

Start free and pay only when a specific limit slows you down: unlimited generations on MagicSchool Plus, Google and Microsoft exports on Diffit, or unlimited sessions on Curipod. A paid plan should remove one named bottleneck, not just feel more complete.
